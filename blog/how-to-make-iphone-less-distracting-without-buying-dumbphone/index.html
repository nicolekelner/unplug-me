How to make your iPhone less distracting without buying a dumb phone

A simple setup for keeping smartphone essentials and reducing reflexive app use.

By Nicole Kelner · October 7, 2026

Keep your iPhone and change the parts that pull at your attention: the first Home Screen, unnecessary alerts, and instant access to feeds. You can get some of the calm of a basic phone while retaining maps, camera, calls, and other tools you need.

Define your essential phone

List the tools you rely on in a normal week. Calls and messages are obvious for many people, but your list may include maps, a transit pass, banking, accessibility tools, or music. Do not build a setup that makes your daily life harder than it needs to be.

Put those tools somewhere easy to reach. Move feed-based apps away from the first Home Screen so opening your phone does not immediately suggest a scroll.

Reduce visual and notification noise

Use a simple wallpaper, remove widgets you rarely act on, and turn off nonessential alerts. These changes will not block an app, but they make the phone less demanding when you pick it up for another reason.

Choose the alerts you keep deliberately. Test important calls and messages after changing settings.

Add the right kind of limit

For a time-based rule, try Apple’s App Limits or Downtime. If you want access tied to a place and action, Unplug Me uses your charger. Plug in for a timed session with distracting apps; unplug and they lock again.

Different boundaries solve different problems. You can use a simple Home Screen even if you do not need an app blocker.

Run a normal-day test

Use the setup on a workday, an evening, and a trip across town. Check that navigation, communication, and other essentials still work. Adjust any blocked app that you genuinely need.

An iPhone with boundaries is still an iPhone. The aim is to make its useful functions easy and its distracting functions more deliberate.

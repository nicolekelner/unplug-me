How to stop doomscrolling without deleting your apps

Make checking in a choice, not a reflex.

By Nicole Kelner, creator of Unplug Me · Updated October 7, 2026

To reduce doomscrolling without deleting your apps, make opening them a deliberate decision. Identify your scrolling moments, remove unnecessary prompts, choose a place to check in, and give each session an ending.

1. Notice the moment before the scroll

Pick one situation where you tend to lose track of time: waking up, waiting for dinner, taking a work break, or getting into bed. Focus on that one moment first rather than trying to change your whole day.

Ask what you were looking for when you opened the app. A message from a friend, a little rest, and an update on the news are different needs. An endless feed may not be the best way to meet all of them.

2. Remove a few prompts

Move the app off your first Home Screen and review its notifications. Keep alerts that serve a clear purpose; switch off the ones that mostly pull you into browsing. These changes create a quieter starting point, even though they don’t prevent you from opening the app.

3. Give scrolling a place

Choose somewhere you’re comfortable checking your apps, such as a chair beside a charging outlet. Keep that place separate from somewhere you want to protect, like your bed or the dinner table.

Unplug Me makes a charger part of the boundary: plug in to unlock distracting apps for a timed session, then unplug to block them again. A dedicated spot can make that interaction more intentional. It is a boundary you set up for yourself, not a promise that you can never bypass it.

4. Decide on an ending before you begin

Before opening a feed, decide what you came to do. Reply to the message. Check one update. Then leave. If you want to browse, decide how long the session should last.

With Unplug Me, distracting apps lock again when the session timer ends. That gives a session an outside stopping point instead of relying entirely on noticing that you’ve had enough. See how timed sessions work.

5. Have something small ready instead

When a familiar scrolling moment comes around, it helps to have an easy alternative: a book beside the couch, a notebook on your desk, or a short walk around the block. Choose something you actually want to do, not another task you feel guilty about avoiding.

For a break, the replacement can simply be a break. You don’t have to make every recovered minute productive.

6. Adjust the boundary after a few days

Look at which moments felt easier and which still pulled you into a feed. Did you put the charger somewhere convenient for intentional use? Did you leave an important tool blocked? Make the setup practical enough that you want to keep using it.

These are practical suggestions, not a guaranteed reduction in screen time. If you want a broader reset of your phone’s setup, try our guide to making your iPhone more like a dumb phone.

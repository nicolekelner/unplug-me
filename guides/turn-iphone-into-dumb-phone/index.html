How to turn your iPhone into a dumb phone

Keep the useful parts. Lose the endless scroll.

By Nicole Kelner, creator of Unplug Me · Updated October 7, 2026

You can make your iPhone behave more like a dumb phone by keeping essential tools easy to reach and adding boundaries around distracting apps. Start with a simpler Home Screen, fewer notifications, and a clear way to limit access to feeds.

1. Decide what your phone is for

Write a short list of things you want your phone to help you do: call a friend, find your way home, take a photo, listen to music, or show a train ticket. Those are your essentials. Your list may also include work tools, accessibility apps, or services you rely on each day.

The point isn’t to make your phone useless. It’s to separate useful actions from the apps you open without meaning to.

2. Make the useful things easier to find

Keep your first Home Screen focused on your essentials. Move feed-based apps away from that screen so they aren’t the first thing you see whenever you unlock your phone. A quiet wallpaper and fewer widgets can also make the screen feel less busy.

Moving an icon does not block an app. Think of this as removing a reminder to scroll, rather than creating a firm limit.

3. Reduce the invitations to come back

Review notifications for the apps that interrupt you most. Keep the alerts you actually need, and turn off the ones that mainly invite you to browse. Make changes one app at a time so you don’t accidentally silence something important.

If you need to be reachable for family or work, build around that requirement. A simpler phone should fit your life.

4. Put a boundary around the distracting apps

Choose when and where you want to use social feeds, entertainment, or other distracting apps. You might check them at a particular time or in a particular place. The useful question is: what will interrupt the automatic reach for my phone?

Unplug Me adds a physical cue. It uses your charger to unlock distracting apps for a timed session. When you unplug or the session ends, those apps lock again. Calls, texts, and your chosen everyday tools can stay available. Read how charger-based blocking works.

5. Try one ordinary day

Test your setup on a normal day before relying on it for travel or an important event. Check that you can reach the tools you need. Notice when you instinctively reach for a blocked app, and decide what you’d rather do at that moment.

Keep what helps. Adjust what creates unnecessary friction. The goal is a phone that serves you, not a perfect streak.

Is this the same as buying a dumb phone?

No. Your iPhone remains a smartphone, and software restrictions are not the same as removing its capabilities. This approach lets you keep familiar tools while making distracting apps less accessible.

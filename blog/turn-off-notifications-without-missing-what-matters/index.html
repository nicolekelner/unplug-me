UNPLUG ME JOURNAL / FOCUS

How to turn off distracting notifications without missing what matters

Sort alerts by urgency and keep the people and services you depend on reachable.

By Nicole Kelner · October 7, 2026

Start by turning off alerts that ask you to browse, while keeping alerts that require a timely response. Review one app at a time and test essential contacts so a quieter phone does not become an unreliable one.

Sort by action, not by app

Ask what you do when each notification arrives. A person asking a direct question might need a reply. A promotion, suggested post, or reminder to open a feed can usually wait until you decide to check the app.

One app can send both useful and distracting alerts. Review its alert categories if it offers them, rather than silencing it blindly.

Keep the urgent channels

Make a short list of people and services that must reach you: family, work, a delivery you are waiting for, or a security alert. Keep these routes working and test them after changing settings.

Apple’s Screen Time and Focus settings serve different jobs. Screen Time limits access; Focus manages when notifications interrupt you. You can use either or both.

Create a time to check less urgent things

Turning off an alert does not mean ignoring someone. Decide when you will check messages, news, or social apps. Put the plan on a calendar if that helps you remember.

If opening the app still turns into a long session, add a separate access boundary. Unplug Me uses your charger and a timer for that part of the problem.

Adjust when your life changes

A class chat may be urgent during a project and quiet afterward. A family contact may need a special exception for a few weeks. Revisit the list when your commitments change.

The useful result is fewer unnecessary interruptions while the important paths still work.
